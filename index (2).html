About

I'm studying integrated business management at Dong-A University, which means I spend my weeks on accounting, marketing, operations and strategy rather than one narrow specialism. What pulled me in was finance — the part where a company's decisions show up as numbers you can actually read.

Alongside coursework I'm teaching myself to code. Not to become a developer, but because the analysts I want to learn from all work in spreadsheets, Python and SQL, and I'd rather pull my own data than wait for someone to send it to me.

Why games

I've spent a lot of hours in FromSoftware games. Sekiro taught me that a wall you can't get past is usually a pattern you haven't read properly yet, and Elden Ring is the rare case of a studio betting on something difficult and being rewarded for it at enormous scale.

That bet is what interests me professionally. Games are a business of long development cycles, uncertain hits and audiences who notice everything — which makes the financial side genuinely hard and genuinely interesting. Studio economics, live-service revenue, the difference between a game that sells and a game that keeps selling: that's what I want to spend my career analysing, and what I want to contribute back to the community that got me here.
